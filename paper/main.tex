\begin{abstract}
Free-form language-guided grasping in clutter requires more than choosing the object named by an instruction. A robot must resolve the intended target, decide whether the target or a visible blocker should be grasped first, and produce a mask that is consistent with that decision. Recent task-oriented grasping and vision-language-action systems improve language-conditioned grasp prediction, but aggregate success rates often hide whether failures arise from target identity, obstruction reasoning, or segmentation. We present Evidence-Gated Hierarchical Consensus Routing (EGHC), an inference-time routing contract for FreeGrasp-style marked candidates rather than an end-to-end action foundation model. EGHC builds a deployment-available evidence packet, gates direct, target-first, and blocker-first reasoning through hierarchical consensus, and applies point-constrained mask repair. On 900 FreeGraspData instructions, the frozen no-ground-truth EGHC3\_v3 configuration achieves 60.89\% reasoning success rate (RSR) and 55.22\% segmentation success rate (SSR). Against a controlled same-cache S0 baseline, EGHC3\_v3 improves SSR by +7.67 pp (95\% CI [4.19, 11.33]) and yields a smaller positive RSR delta of +3.00 pp (95\% CI [0.00, 5.95]). The results support auditable target/blocker routing as a practical way to localize failures in cluttered language-guided grasping, with the strongest controlled evidence in SSR and a remaining hard-scene bottleneck in blocker and visibility reasoning.
\end{abstract}
