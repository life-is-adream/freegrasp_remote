\begin{abstract}
Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have shown promise for robotic grasping from free-form language instructions, but existing approaches struggle with hard scenes involving heavy occlusion and object ambiguity. We present FreeGrasp++, a protocol-aware extension that introduces three key improvements: (1) a \emph{Unified Hard-Safe Protocol} that defaults to conservative blocker-first actions when uncertain, (2) an \emph{Evidence-Gated Hard-Safe Cascade (EGHC)} with deterministic guard routing and conditional adjudication, and (3) \emph{point-constrained mask repair} for robust segmentation. On the FreeGraspData benchmark, FreeGrasp++ achieves 67.4\% RSR and 60.1\% SSR, representing absolute improvements of 9.5\% and 12.5\% over the original FreeGrasp. For hard and medium subsets, ground-truth semantic and geometric features serve as auxiliary VLM input (oracle features); a deployable configuration without oracle features achieves 61.0\% RSR. Ablation studies confirm that hard-safe fallback is the most critical component, contributing +9.5\% RSR improvement. Our results demonstrate that explicit protocol engineering with uncertainty-aware fallback can substantially improve VLM-based robotic grasping without additional model training.
\end{abstract}
