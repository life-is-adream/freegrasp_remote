\begin{abstract}
Free-form language-guided grasping requires a robot to resolve the instructed target, determine whether that target is immediately graspable, and select either the target or a visible blocker as the first grasp. Existing vision-language pipelines often collapse these decisions into a direct object answer, which makes target confusion, blocker mistakes, and mask failures difficult to separate. We present Evidence-Gated Hierarchical Consensus Routing (EGHC), a deployable extension of the FreeGrasp decision contract. EGHC builds an inference-time evidence packet from marked Molmo candidates, predicted spatial and mask evidence, and route-specific consistency checks; it then gates direct, target-first, and blocker-first reasoning through a hierarchical consensus procedure and applies point-constrained mask repair. On 900 FreeGraspData instructions, the frozen EGHC3\_v3 configuration achieves 60.89\% reasoning success rate (RSR) and 55.22\% segmentation success rate (SSR), descriptively exceeding the reported original FreeGrasp GPT-4o baseline by approximately +13.6 pp RSR and +17.0 pp SSR. Against the controlled same-cache S0 FreeGrasp-protocol baseline, EGHC3\_v3 improves SSR by +7.67 pp (95\% CI [4.19, 11.33]) and RSR by +3.00 pp (95\% CI [0.00, 5.95]) while using no ground-truth features or benchmark difficulty labels. An oracle S1 upper bound reaches 67.44\% RSR and 60.11\% SSR, indicating remaining headroom from better visibility and blocker evidence. The results support auditable evidence-gated routing over direct VLM answering, while showing that hard-scene blocker and instance ambiguity remain open bottlenecks.
\end{abstract}
