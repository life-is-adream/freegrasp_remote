\section{Related Work}

\subsection{VLM-based Robotic Grasping}

Recent work has explored using Vision-Language Models for robotic grasping tasks. ThinkGrasp~\cite{thinkgrasp} uses GPT-4o for strategic part grasping in heavy clutter by reasoning about obstructing objects over multiple steps. FreeGrasp~\cite{freegrasp} introduced mark-based visual prompting with Molmo~\cite{molmo} for object detection and GPT-4o for grasp reasoning, achieving strong performance while exposing the need for clearer target/blocker failure localization. MOKA~\cite{moka} proposes mark-based visual prompting for open-world robotic manipulation. Our work builds on these approaches by making the target/blocker decision contract explicit and auditable.

\subsection{Structured Reasoning Protocols for VLMs}

Structured reasoning has emerged as a key technique for improving LLM and VLM reliability. Chain-of-thought prompting~\cite{chainofthought} improves reasoning by decomposing complex tasks into intermediate steps, but free-form rationales are difficult to verify inside a robotic pipeline. Set-of-Mark prompting~\cite{setofmark} enhances visual grounding by annotating objects with unique identifiers. EGHC follows this structured-output direction, but the structure is task-specific: it separates target resolution, blocker evidence, route selection, and first-grasp planning.

\subsection{Spatial Reasoning for Robotics}

Spatial reasoning is critical for robotic grasping in cluttered scenes. SpatialVLM~\cite{spatialvlm} endows vision-language models with spatial reasoning capabilities through training on spatial data. RoboPoint~\cite{robopoint} predicts spatial affordances for robotic manipulation. Grounding DINO~\cite{groundingdino} enables open-set object detection with language queries. Our approach does not claim to solve 3D spatial reasoning; instead, it gates and audits the evidence available to a marked-candidate FreeGrasp pipeline.

\subsection{Occlusion Handling in Robotic Manipulation}

Understanding object occlusion is critical for robotic grasping in cluttered scenes. Prior work has explored occlusion-aware grasp planning~\cite{occlusion} and visual manipulation relationship networks~\cite{relationship}, but these approaches typically require explicit 3D reconstruction or relationship modeling. EGHC offers a complementary route: it does not replace geometric occlusion modeling, but exposes blocker and visibility decisions so that failures can be localized and future predicted 3D evidence can be inserted into the same decision contract.
