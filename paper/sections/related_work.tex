\section{Related Work}

\subsection{VLM-based Robotic Grasping}

Recent work has explored using Vision-Language Models for robotic grasping tasks. ThinkGrasp~\cite{thinkgrasp} uses GPT-4o for strategic part grasping in clutter, but struggles with hard scenes where objects are heavily occluded. FreeGrasp~\cite{freegrasp} introduced mark-based visual prompting with Molmo~\cite{molmo} for object detection and GPT-4o for grasp reasoning, achieving strong performance but with limited robustness in occluded scenes. MOKA~\cite{moka} proposes mark-based visual prompting for open-world robotic manipulation. Our work builds on these approaches by introducing protocol-aware reasoning that explicitly handles uncertainty through hard-safe fallback.

\subsection{Protocol Engineering for LLMs}

Protocol engineering has emerged as a key technique for improving LLM reliability. Chain-of-thought prompting~\cite{chainofthought} improves reasoning by decomposing complex tasks into intermediate steps. Set-of-Mark prompting~\cite{setofmark} enhances visual grounding by annotating objects with unique identifiers. Structured output formats enforce consistency and enable error detection. Our \emph{Unified Hard-Safe Protocol} extends this by incorporating domain-specific safety rules that default to conservative actions under uncertainty, which is particularly important for robotic manipulation where incorrect actions can be costly.

\subsection{Spatial Reasoning for Robotics}

Spatial reasoning is critical for robotic grasping in cluttered scenes. SpatialVLM~\cite{spatialvlm} endows vision-language models with spatial reasoning capabilities through training on spatial data. RoboPoint~\cite{robopoint} predicts spatial affordances for robotic manipulation. Grounding DINO~\cite{groundingdino} enables open-set object detection with language queries. Our approach leverages the VLM's implicit understanding of spatial relationships through structured reasoning prompts, avoiding the need for explicit 3D modeling or additional training.

\subsection{Occlusion Handling in Robotic Manipulation}

Understanding object occlusion is critical for robotic grasping in cluttered scenes. Prior work has explored occlusion-aware grasp planning~\cite{occlusion} and visual manipulation relationship networks~\cite{relationship}, but these approaches typically require explicit 3D reconstruction or relationship modeling. Our approach leverages the VLM's implicit understanding of occlusion through structured reasoning prompts and hard-safe fallback, avoiding the need for explicit 3D modeling while maintaining robustness in challenging scenes.
