\section{Conclusion}

We presented Evidence-Gated Hierarchical Consensus Routing for free-form language-guided grasping. The method changes the FreeGrasp decision contract from a direct object answer into an auditable target/blocker process with deployment-available evidence, route-specific planning, hierarchical consensus, and point-constrained mask repair.

On 900 FreeGraspData instructions, the frozen EGHC3\_v3 configuration achieved 60.89\% RSR and 55.22\% SSR without ground-truth features or benchmark difficulty labels. This improves the reported original FreeGrasp GPT-4o baseline by approximately +13.6 pp RSR and +17.0 pp SSR, and improves the controlled S0 original-protocol baseline by +3.00 pp RSR and +7.67 pp SSR. The separated S1 oracle result of 67.44\% RSR and 60.11\% SSR shows remaining headroom but is not a deployable method result.

\textbf{Limitations.} EGHC still struggles in hard scenes, where blocker and visibility inference remain unreliable. Easy-scene failures increasingly reflect instance-mask ambiguity, whereas hard-scene failures often occur before segmentation. The present evidence is benchmark RSR/SSR, not a full real-robot sequence success study, and paired bootstrap confidence intervals still need to be added before final submission.

\textbf{Future work.} The next step is to strengthen predicted visibility and blocker evidence, add statistically paired confidence intervals, and validate whether RSR/SSR gains translate to multi-step real-robot grasp execution.
